Air Blast Attenuation
Air Blast Attenuation automatically searches for anomalous
energy on a trace-by-trace basis using a pilot velocity, a
relative noise amplitude threshold, and an approximate
energy envelope width.

Theory
Strong energy from sources, such as shot-generated air
blasts, can contaminate seismic data with very high
amplitude, broad band noise. This noise must typically be
muted prior to subsequent processing, such as ﬁltering and
deconvolution, since these processes can propagate the
strong noise bursts in proportion to the length of the ﬁlter
operators. Unfortunately, such surgical mutes can produce
undesired and sometimes unexpected results in decon
design, amplitude scaling, residual statics and other
processes. In addition, these noise trains can be very strong
on some records but virtually non-existent just a few records
away. In the case of air blasts resulting from hole blow on
poorly tamped dynamite shotholes, the air blast can appear
and disappear on a shot-by-shot basis.
Air Blast Attenuation attempts to address these problems
by providing you with a relatively automated air blast
attenuation tool. It will automatically seek out anomalous
energy on a trace-by-trace basis, given a pilot velocity, a
relative noise amplitude threshold, and an approximate
energy envelope width. This strong energy, if found, can be
either surgically muted with appropriate edge tapering, or
attenuated down to an amplitude level similar to the
surrounding data. If the trace does not appear to be
contaminated by noise, the process will leave the trace
untouched. Since Air Blast Attenuation is a single trace
process, the trace-to-trace amplitude variations are
approximately preserved. This is important if subsequent
processing attempts to measure and preserve true amplitude
relationships.

Usage
Air Blast Attenuation can be used to attenuate other linear
noise trains that are strong relative to the surrounding data.

Geometry must have been assigned prior to the application of
this process. In general, input traces should have True
Amplitude Recovery processing; however, reasonable
results may be obtained if an AGC scalar length is long
relative to the width of the energy envelope of the noise train.
The run times for this tool are normally less that of an AGC,
unless the automatic picking option is used in combination
with the Filter to apply to enhance air blast energy
parameter. If this ﬁlter is speciﬁed, run times will normally be
about half that of a simple bandpass ﬁlter.

Parameters
Approximate velocity of energy to be attenuated
Enter the approximate velocity of the noise that you want to
attenuate. This process assumes the noise is approximately
constant in velocity. If this is not the case, consider using
several repeated copies of Air Blast Attenuation in your ﬂow
using the automatic noise search and detection option.
Specify the velocity of each noise train you wish to attenuate
for each copy of Air Blast Attenuation in the ﬂow. This will
work for air blasts, but not for ground roll. Therefore, it
doesn’t work well on noise trains that are extremely
dispersive.
Start time (ms) at zero offset
Enter the start time of the noise train at 0 offset. The default
is 0 ms for air blasts.
Type of energy attenuation
Select the type of energy attenuation from:
• Muting applies a sample-zeroing surgical mute with
edge tapers to the air blast energy.
• Attenuation applies an inverse amplitude scalar to
attenuate the air blast energy. The inverse scalar is computed dynamically based on an estimate of the average
amplitude of the samples in a window just above and
below the time window that is to be attenuated.

Taper ramp length for surgical mute
This appears if Muting to Type of energy attenuation.
Enter the time in ms over which to ramp on and off
amplitudes for surgical mutes.
Automatically search for and detect noise?
Select Yes to automatically determine whether or not a noise
train exists on a particular trace based on the threshold
value. If detected, it then picks the time boundaries for noise
editing.
Time gate width of air blast energy to attenuate
This appears if No to Automatically search for and detect
noise. Enter the energy envelope width, in ms, for the strong
noise amplitudes.
Note: This process cannot accept a value that is longer than
one-ﬁfth the trace length.
Maximum air blast energy envelope width
This appears if Yes to Automatically search for and detect
noise. Enter the maximum energy envelope width, in ms, for
the strong air blast amplitudes. This process won’t be able to
pick any noise train longer than this value, therefore it is
better to specify a value that’s larger than one that is too
small.
Note: This process cannot accept a value that is longer than
one-ﬁfth the trace length.
Filter to apply to enhance air blast energy
This appears if Yes to Automatically seach for and detect
noise. Enter the ﬁlter as four frequencies (0%, 100%, 100%,
0%) or a blank string for no ﬁlter to help detect air blast
energy start and end times.
Note: This ﬁlter is used to help pick the start and end times
of the noise to be attenuated and has no effect on the
frequency content of the seismic trace output from this
process. Applying this ﬁlter has a signiﬁcant affect on run
times, so don’t use the ﬁlter if you don’t need it. However, if
the air blast noise has signiﬁcant energy outside the normal

Air blast picking amplitude ratio threshold
This appears if Yes to Automatically seach for and detect
noise. The automatic picking of noise trains is based on the
ratio of the average amplitudes to the amplitudes of the
surrounding data. The ratio speciﬁes the value of this
parameter.
Percentage to expand picked air blast energy envelopes
This appears if Yes to Automatically seach for and detect
noise. Detected noise blocks are expanded by this percentage
of their length before editing. If you specify 20%, the top
arrival time of the noise burst will be shifted up by 10% of the
detected noise block’s length, and the bottom arrival time of
the noise burst will be shifted down by 10% of the detected
noise block’s length. Since the edges of the noise bursts can’t
be exactly detected, it is usually better to use a higher
threshold parameter, such as 2.5, combined with an
expansion percentage, such as 30%.
Minimum detected air blast energy envelope length
This appears if Yes to Automatically seach for and detect
noise. Enter the minimum length in ms of automatically
detected air blast energy. Suspected noise windows shorter
than this length are ignored.
