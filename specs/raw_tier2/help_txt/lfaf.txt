Surface Wave Noise Attenuation
Surface Wave Noise Attenuation attenuates surface-wave
noise by forming low-frequency arrays.

Theory
Given the surface velocity and the frequency cutoff of the
noise, Surface Wave Noise Attenuation transforms the data
from the time-space domain to the frequency-space domain,
where it performs a frequency dependent mix of adjacent
traces. At each frequency the number of traces to mix is
determined by the relation
mix = velocity / (frequency * trace spacing)
Frequency components higher than the cutoff frequency
remain unchanged. Finally, the data are transformed back to
the time-space domain.
Surface Wave Noise Attenuation operates on the data in
panels, which are overlapped and merged together. Each
panel normally contains Panel size traces. The ﬁrst and last
Panel edge traces overlap with adjacent panels. Within the
panel edges, Panel mix traces from adjacent panels are
mixed and output. Remaining traces in the edges are
discarded. For example,
Panel size = 12, Panel edge = 3, Panel mix = 1
OOOOOOOOOMXX
XXMOOOOOOMXX
XXMOOOOOOOOO
O = Output, M = Mixed, X = Discarded

Panels come in to play when the maximum number of traces
per ensemble (MAXDTR) is 1 (MAXDTR = 1 is the normal
value for poststack data) or when MAXDTR is greater than
panel size. If MAXDTR > 1, panels do not cross ensemble
boundaries. If the number of traces in an ensemble is less
than the panel size, the ensemble is processed as a single
panel with no overlap or mix.

Usage
You can run this process on both prestack and poststack
data.
In some cases, to achieve better noise suppression, it is
recommended to apply gain to the data prior to noise
attenuation. The high cutoff frequency can be obtained from
amplitude spectra.

Parameters
Velocity
Enter the apparent velocity of the noise wave to attenuate in
ft./s or m/s.
Trace spacing
Enter the trace spacing in ft. or m. Enter 0. to have the
program calculate the average trace spacing for each panel
from OFFSET values in the trace headers.
Note: the program assumes that all traces in a panel are
equally spaced. Noise attenuation decreases if trace spacing
varies within a panel and where there are gaps in the data.
Low frequency (Hz)
Enter the lowest frequency in the noise wave to attenuate.
Generally this should be zero. However, at zero frequency the
number of traces mixed is always equal to the panel size.
High frequency (Hz)
Enter the highest frequency in the noise wave to attenuate.
On most data it should not exceed 25-30 Hz.
Blend width (Hz)
Enter a bandwidth to blend up to the high frequency to
preserve continuity at the cutoff.
Set trace mix limit?
Select Yes to put an upper limit on the number of traces
mixed.

Maximum number of traces to mix
This appears if Yes to Set trace mix limit. Enter the largest
number of traces to mix. The number must be odd.
Panel size
Enter the number of traces in each panel.
Panel edge
Enter the number of traces at the edge of each panel that are
inﬂuenced by the edge effect and must be overlapped. Panel
edge must be less than one-half Panel size. Panel edge
should be one-half of the maximum trace mix. If it is less,
noise attenuation may decrease at panel boundaries.
Panel mix
Enter the number of traces in the overlap zones between
panels to be mixed with adjacent panels’ overlap zones and
output. Panel mix cannot be greater than Panel edge.
